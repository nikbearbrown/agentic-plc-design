# Tool intake sheet

## Executive summary

A one-page checklist for deciding whether a new AI tool, or a vendor's claim about one, deserves any of your time or your company's data. Use it in Module 12 and every time a new product shows up in your inbox. It exists because the tools will keep changing and your judgment is the only part that carries over.

## The sheet

Copy this into a file named `intake-<tool>-<date>.md` and fill it in. Leave a line blank rather than guessing.

1. **What is it, in one sentence, without the vendor's adjectives?**
2. **What claim is being made?** Quote it. Who made it and where?
3. **What task of mine would it replace or speed up?** Name a real task from this month.
4. **What does it need?** Inputs, standards in digital form, licenses, cost per seat.
5. **Where does my data go?** Local, vendor cloud, a third-party model. Does company policy allow it? Who said so?
6. **Can I test it on synthetic or public material first?** Do that.
7. **The oracle question:** how will I know it is wrong? What checker, test or reference do I have that does not depend on the tool?
8. **Run the test.** Same task, same input, three times. Record what it got right, wrong and could not determine.
9. **Failure mode:** when it was wrong, was it obvious or plausible? Plausible-wrong is the dangerous kind.
10. **What stays human?** Name the decision or signature that remains yours.
11. **Verdict:** adopt / pilot on non-critical work / watch / ignore. Date it. Set a review date 90 days out.

## Rule of thumb

A claim about speed ("3 to 5 times faster") tells you nothing until you know what was measured, on what, by whom. Measure your own task.
